How should salefarmgate calculate
sharing profit?

Two valid accrual methods are on the table. We'll show why the financial difference is negligible — making this a workflow choice, not a revenue decision.

The problem

When a customer pays back an installment, salefarmgate can apply it fees-first (kill accrued sharing-profit, then reduce principal) or principal-first (reduce principal, settle the fee at trade close). Same total fee in the end — different timing. Which method should the team use?

The answer (preview)

On a typical trade the gap between the two methods is < 0.1 % of principal — a few million VND. This is not a revenue lever. Pick whichever method fits the finance and customer-ops workflow better.

1 · Backstory — why the same word means two things

farmnet and salefarmgate define "outstanding" differently

Brief context before we dig into salefarmgate. Full details in the cross-system companion deck.

farmnet

Outstanding = principal + margin + VAT, fused into one number at SO creation.

salefarmgate

Outstanding = principal only (cash gap). Sharing-profit lives in a separate, time-accrued column.

Why this matters here

This deck focuses on the salefarmgate side: how it calculates outstanding and sharing-profit, and the operational choice between two valid methods. The cross-system mismatch is real and structural — but separate from today's question.

2 · The salefarmgate trade timeline

Goods travel for ~32 days. TC's cash is out for ~56.

salefarmgate trades have two clocks. The physical clock (order → goods arrive) drives operations. The cash clock (first vendor pay → last customer receipt) drives interest cost. The cash clock is what the calculation methods live on.

3 · Two ways to accrue sharing-profit in salefarmgate

Method A vs Method B — both are fine

When a customer's installment arrives, does it kill the accrued fee first, or reduce principal first? Different timing — almost identical totals.

Method A — Fees first

Each payment kills accrued fee, then knocks down principal. Fee revenue spreads smoothly across the trade.

Best when

Smoother P&L reporting — fee revenue period by period.

Method B — Principal first

Each payment reduces principal. Fee accrues alone, settled in one piece at trade close.

Best when

Simpler customer installments — one final fee invoice.

Business implication

The method gap as a % of principal depends only on time, not size. On a p50-duration trade (56 d) the gap is ~ 0.06 % of principal regardless of trade size. On a p90-duration trade (104 d) it's ~ 0.22 %. Even on the largest realistic trade (p90 size × p90 duration), the absolute gap is under 8 M VND — not material to any business decision.

4 · The business tradeoff

Pick the method that fits the team's workflow

Method A · Fees first

+ Smoother P&L — fee revenue recognized period by period.

− Each payment splits into fee + principal. Two ledger lines per installment.

Method B · Principal first

+ Simple customer installments — every payment is principal. One fee invoice at the end.

− Fee revenue lumps at trade close, not period by period.

The honest framing

Same total fee on a typical trade. Choose between smoother P&L (A) and simpler ops (B). Either is defensible.

Footnote

Gap reaches ~ 1 % of principal on stuck trades. Re-visit if long-tail / default volume grows.

Single takeaway

The two methods produce essentially the same total sharing-profit on a typical salefarmgate trade. This is a workflow choice, not a revenue lever. Pick whichever fits the finance and customer-ops teams better.

What is "Outstanding" —
and why it's not just cash in and out

Sharing Profit tracks what cash has actually moved. Outstanding tracks what TC is still committed to — and that requires looking at Bills and Invoices too.

Sharing Profit

Accrues on actual cash movements only.

Out − In

Clock starts when TC pays vendors. Stops when customers pay TC. Simple.

Outstanding

Tracks TC's total capital exposure — cash and committed positions.

(Out − Bill) + (Inv − In)

Subtracts vendor bills from cash paid out (unpaid bills reduce net deployment); adds customer invoices not yet collected.

The core difference

Sharing Profit only cares about cash that has actually moved. Outstanding must also account for capital that is committed but not yet settled — vendor bills TC owes and customer invoices TC is owed. Ignoring these gives a misleading picture of how much capital TC has in play.

1 · Why cash alone is not enough for Outstanding

Bills and Invoices sit between commitment and settlement

A vendor bill means TC has taken on an obligation — even if no cash has moved yet. A customer invoice means TC has earned a receivable — even if the customer hasn't paid.

The problem with cash-only

If TC buys goods on a 30-day vendor bill, a cash-only view shows zero outstanding until the bill is paid — even though TC has already taken on the obligation. Similarly, once TC invoices a customer, that receivable exists and should be counted in TC's outstanding position — but cash-only waits for the cash transfer to happen first.

2 · The formula

Outstanding = (Out − Bill) + (Inv − In)

Two components, each measuring the unsettled gap on one side of the trade.

Outstanding  =  ( Out − Bill )  +  ( Inv − In )
net vendor cash deployed  +  uncollected customer receivables
Out
Cash out to vendors
Actual buy payments TC has already made to suppliers. Cash that has left TC's account.
Bill
Total vendor bills received
Cumulative value of all vendor bills issued to TC. Paired with Out, the gap (Out − Bill) shows how much TC still owes vendors or has overpaid in advance.
Inv
Customer invoices issued
Sales invoices TC has sent to customers. Customers owe this to TC — cash hasn't arrived yet.
In
Cash in from customers
Actual sales receipts TC has already collected. Cash that has entered TC's account.
Component 1 · Out − Bill

TC's net vendor position: cash paid minus total bills received. Negative means TC still owes vendors (common mid-trade); positive means TC has an advance credit with no bill yet. Zero means vendor side is fully settled.

Component 2 · Inv − In

TC's uncollected customer receivables: what customers have been invoiced but haven't paid yet. TC has delivered the value and is owed this amount.

3 · Why we propose this formula

Four reasons the accrual view is the right one

1 · Bills create real obligations

When TC issues or receives a vendor bill, TC has legally committed to that payment. Excluding it understates TC's true liability — the cash will go out, the obligation exists now.

2 · Invoices complete the outstanding picture

Once TC invoices a customer, TC has a legal claim on that money. It should be captured in Outstanding immediately — waiting for cash arrival leaves the receivable invisible and understates what TC is still owed.

3 · Cash-only is a lagging indicator

Cash movements happen after commitments. A cash-only view always trails reality — it tells you what TC paid, not what TC owes. Outstanding should be a current snapshot, not a rearview mirror.

4 · Sharing Profit already handles cash

Sharing Profit (Out − In) already captures the time-weighted cash exposure. Outstanding should be the complementary view — what's committed but not yet settled. Each formula answers a different question.

In plain terms

Sharing Profit asks: "How long has TC's cash been out in the market?"
Outstanding asks: "How much of TC's capital is still in play right now — cash deployed plus commitments not yet settled?"
The formula (Out − Bill) + (Inv − In) answers the second question correctly.

Reading the signal

In the happy case Outstanding falls cleanly to 0 at vendor payment. In the loss case it stops at +25M — the 25M disputed receivable TC cannot yet collect. A trade that doesn't close to zero has an unresolved position. The formula makes that visible; cash-only does not.

6 · Alignment question — Farmgate vs Farmnet

Farmnet bundles Sharing Profit into Outstanding. Should we align — or name the difference?

TC's formula separates capital exposure from earned profit. Farmnet treats them as one number. Before we ship, this needs a decision.

Farmgate's proposed formula
(Out − Bill) + (Inv − In)
  • Tracks capital exposure only — bills committed, invoices issued, cash in/out
  • Sharing Profit is a separate metric: Out − In
  • A fully settled, profitable trade closes to exactly 0
  • Profit sits outside Outstanding — it's earned, not outstanding
Farmnet's Outstanding
TC Outstanding + Sharing Profit
  • Bundles capital exposure and earned profit into one balance
  • Sharing Profit is absorbed into Outstanding — not tracked separately
  • Profit owed back to TC is treated as part of the open balance
Open question — two paths

Name the combined metric. Keep Farmgate Outstanding as the pure accrual formula. Define a new term — e.g. Net Settlement Balance or Total TC Position — for Outstanding + Sharing Profit. Clearer semantics; requires agreeing on a name and explaining the split to Farmnet.

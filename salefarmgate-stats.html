The takeaway

A typical trade closes in ~32 days. The typical-large trade (p90) uses ~50–60% of the customer's credit line on both sides — well within headroom. Only the top 1% crosses the line. A single SO does not saturate a credit; credit accumulates utilization across many trades over time.

D · Interest Calculation

How big is the method-choice lever?

Two ways to recognize interest revenue when customers pay back. Same total accrued — different timing of when principal falls. Question: does the choice meaningfully move the number?

Method A — Fees first

Each customer payment kills accrued interest first, then reduces principal. Fees show up smoothly throughout the trade.

Best when

You want smooth fee-revenue recognition across the trade — better for periodic P&L reporting.

Method B — Principal first

Each payment reduces principal directly. All fees accrue separately and are settled at the end (balloon).

Best when

You want simple customer-facing installments — every payment is "principal repayment."

Two scenarios that bracket the answer

A 5 B VND credit line at 2.5 % per 30 days, paid back in 3 installments. Numbers in the table below are total interest revenue per trade.

The answer

For a typical trade the method choice is worth ~3 M VND per trade (0.034 % of principal per 30 days — about 1.4 % of TC's 2.5 % rate). For a stuck near-default trade it's worth ~53 M VND (0.142 % per 30 d — 5.7 % of the rate). The lever is small per typical trade but the per-30-day rate quadruples on stuck exposure. The choice is an accounting workflow question for the bulk of the book, but a meaningful one on stuck trades.

Why the per-30-day gap grows: Method A keeps principal slightly higher per installment, so a tiny extra accrual compounds against itself. The effect scales as r² × T × P / 2 — quadratic in rate and linear in time, so the per-period rate scales with T. At 2.5 %/30 d the gap stays under 9 % of total interest even at 225-day exposure.

The per-30-day rate quadruples from typical to worst case (0.034 % → 0.142 %). This isn't compounding interest — it's the second-order Method-A-vs-B effect compounding against itself as the trade stretches. Roughly: doubling the duration doubles the per-30-day gap.

Why it matters

Choosing fees-first is approximately a 0.034 % per 30 days advantage on a typical trade — about 1.4 % of TC's 2.5 % rate. On stuck trades it's 0.142 % per 30 days (5.7 % of the rate), and the per-trade absolute gap reaches ~50 M VND. The lever is small per normal trade but worth flagging as risk-of-default rises.
